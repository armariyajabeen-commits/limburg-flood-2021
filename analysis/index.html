Observed flood versus modelled scenarios

The two are different kinds of evidence and are never mixed. Observed: the maximum flooded extent of July 2021 (4TU.ResearchData), a preliminary reconstruction. Modelled: dike-breach scenarios from the national hazard maps (T = 10, 100, 1,000 and 10,000 years), a design scenario for a different hazard type. The panels show the same 3.6 km window around . The solid blue area is observed; hatched teal areas are modelled.

How many of the 2,033 observed-flooded residential buildings does each scenario catch?

A building counts as caught when its footprint, buffered by the stated distance, touches the modelled flooded area. The modelled polygons have holes at building footprints, so a plain point-in-polygon test is biased. The buffer rule replaces the first version of this test (script 05b), and the 2 m column is the one used throughout.

Scenario water depth at the observed-flooded buildings

These are the depth classes of the modelled scenario at buildings that were flooded in the observed event. They are scenario depths, not observed depths: the observed extent carries no depth at all.

Source: outputs/modelled_depth_at_flooded_residential_buildings.csv
The modelled hazard is for dike-breach scenarios, the observed event was not a breach flood, and the two have a different licence status (the 4TU extent is CC BY 4.0; the scenario layers have an unclear licence). The scenarios are therefore shown as context and as a test of how well modelled maps predict a real event, not as the exposure measured on the site.

    The BAG is today's snapshot: how 2021 was reconstructed

    The BAG registry is served only as it is today, with no historical archive. Buildings and address objects were therefore filtered to an approximate situation on 14 July 2021 from their status, document date and construction year (script 05_building_exposure.py). This is an approximation, and a building demolished before the event but registered with a later document date could be misclassified.

    Source: logs/run.log (05_building_exposure), logs/06.out

    Is a regression on this sample defensible?

    A natural next step is a geographically weighted regression (GWR, MGWR) of the share of residents in the flooded area on neighbourhood characteristics. That test was run on the Limburg 2021 buurten. The answer is no, and the numbers show why.

    Source: logs/07.out, logs/08.out

    Collinearity (VIF) and rank correlation with exposure

    Source: outputs/vif.csv; Spearman recomputed from outputs/buurt_analysis_env.csv on the same sample as script 07. Only elevation relates to exposure (coefficient p < 0.001 in the logit, outputs/logit_any_exposure.csv): buurten that flood are the low ones.
    Conclusion carried by the project report: the exposure and vulnerability map is supported, a stand-alone GWR/MGWR on this sample is not. The units were chosen because they touch the flood, most have zero exposure, and only 193 have complete predictors.

      Reproducibility frictions

        Display note: the layers on the map (modelled areas, depth classes, buildings, land use, soil, roads, water) are simplified copies made by build/build_web_assets.py so a browser can draw them. No figure on this page or on the map is computed from the simplified copies, and the research data were not changed.
